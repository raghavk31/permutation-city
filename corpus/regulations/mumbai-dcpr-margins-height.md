## Side and rear open space by building height

In residential and commercial zones the side and rear open spaces depend on height
(Table A). For buildings up to 32 m, the minimum is 3.6 m for residential and 4.5 m for
commercial buildings, increasing with height (H/5 on small plots, H/4 on plots over
1,000 m² with average width over 20 m, capped at 6 m or 12 m). From 32 m to 70 m the
spaces are H/5 (to 12 m) or H/4 (to 16 m). Above 120 m, 20 m on the larger plots. Dead-wall
sides need less (3.6 m to 9 m). Tables B and C allow smaller margins on payment of a
premium where there is hardship.

## Front setbacks

Table 18 sets front setbacks from the street line: 6 m on express highways and roads wider
than 52 m. Elsewhere in the Island City 3 m for residential and 4.5 m for commercial
buildings; 4.5 m in the suburbs. Industrial zones need more (6 m to 22.5 m). Small
gaothan and koliwada plots under 250 m² need 0 to 1.5 m depending on street width.

## Tower-like buildings

A tower-like structure up to 32 m needs 6 m of open space at ground level. From 32 m to
70 m: 6 m at the rear and one side and 9 m on the road side. Above 70 m: 9 m at ground and
step-backs at upper levels (two up to 70 m, three up to 120 m, four beyond).

## Height and street width

A building's height may not exceed a multiple of the street width plus the front open
space. This copy shows the multiple as "two three times", an amendment printed as both
words; check the notified figure. The limit does not apply where the plot has a 9 m front
open space on roads up to 9 m wide, or 12 m on wider roads, nor to several Regulation 33
redevelopment schemes. High-rise buildings need wider access roads (Table 6): 9 m for
32 to 70 m, 12 m for 70 to 120 m, 18 m above 120 m.

## Using these numbers in Permutation City

If each building cell is read as a whole plot, setbacks set how much of a cell a building
can cover. A 20 m hex cell is about 1,040 m², roughly a 32 m square. With a 3 to 4.5 m
front and 3.6 m side and rear margins, such a plot keeps roughly 55 to 65 per cent
coverage (worked out here, not stated in the regulations); taller buildings need more space and less
coverage. The coverage gene's upper end is the place to apply this. Heights above 32 m
(about 10 storeys) bring fire, access and step-back requirements.
