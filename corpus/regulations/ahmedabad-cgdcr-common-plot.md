## How much common plot a development keeps

Building units of 2,000 m² or more keep a common plot (shared open space) of 10 per cent
of their area (8 per cent in the core walled city and gamtal). Religious, educational,
institutional and assembly uses keep 20 per cent. Industrial units keep none up to
5,000 m², 8 per cent up to 20,000 m², and 1,600 m² plus 5 per cent of the area above
that. Smaller units with buildings over 25 m high keep 10 per cent or 200 m², whichever is
more.

## Shape and use

A common plot may be split, but each piece is at least 200 m² with no side under 10 m
(8 m for affordable housing), and no angle between sides under 60 degrees. It excludes
approaches but may sit in marginal spaces. Little may be built on it: substations, tanks,
pump rooms, security cabins and community facilities, covering at most 15 per cent and up
to 7.5 m high. In residential schemes up to half may be used for parking.

## Using these numbers in Permutation City

The 10 per cent common plot is a floor for open space in Ahmedabad layouts, well under the
engine's green share range. A brief can keep the green share's lower end at 10 per cent or
more and record the common plot as a pattern of small shared spaces (see pattern 61,
Small Public Squares).
