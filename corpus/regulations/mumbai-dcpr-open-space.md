## How much open space a layout keeps

In residential and commercial zones, a layout or plot keeps a share of its area as layout
open space (LOS, called recreational open space, ROS, in the earlier draft):

- 1,001 to 2,500 m²: 15 per cent
- 2,501 to 10,000 m²: 20 per cent
- above 10,000 m²: 25 per cent

The share excludes access roads, internal roads, reservations, DP roads and road
widening. It should be in one place where possible; above 5,000 m² it may be split, but
one piece must be at least 1,000 m². For several redevelopment schemes under Regulation 33
it may be reduced to a minimum the text gives as "8% 10%" (an amendment shown as both
values in this copy; check the notified figure).

## Size, shape and planting

No piece may be smaller than 125 m² or narrower than 7.5 m. If its average width is under
16.6 m, its length may not exceed 2.5 times the average width. It must be reachable from
every building or have its own access. It stays open to the sky and accessible to all
occupants, with 5 indigenous trees per 100 m² of open space planted in the plot (1 per
100 m² where no open space is needed). A 1.5 m unpaved strip along the plot boundary is
kept for groundwater recharge and trees, and does not count towards the open space.

## On the ground or on a podium

At least 60 per cent of the required open space must be on the ground, and at least half
of that on natural ground ("mother earth"), unpaved, so water soaks in. The other 40 per
cent may be on a podium beyond the building line. Remaining paving in the compound should
be perforated so rain percolates.

## Industrial layouts, and what may be built in open space

Industrial plots of 1,000 m² or more keep 15 per cent as open space, with the same planting
rule. Within open space of 400 m² or more, tanks, substations and pump houses may take up
to 10 per cent. Within 1,000 m² or more, sports and club structures may take up to 15 per
cent of floor area (10 per cent footprint), at most ground plus one storey and 8 m high.

## Using these numbers in Permutation City

For a whole-site design the 25 per cent rule is the relevant one, and at least 60 per cent
of it on the ground. The engine's green share gene (10 to 45 per cent of the design ground)
covers it; a brief can raise the lower end of the green share to 15 per cent so every design
keeps the ground-level part. Unpaved, planted open space also counts towards flood storage.
