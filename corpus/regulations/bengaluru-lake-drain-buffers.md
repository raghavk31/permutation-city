## Lakes

- **RMP 2015, 4.12.2 ii (iii):** a 30 m "no development zone" round each lake, measured from
  the lake's boundary in the revenue records, except activities that belong to the lake. It
  may count towards a layout's park reservation.
- **NGT, 4 May 2016** (Forward Foundation v. State of Karnataka): 75 m round lakes and wetlands,
  and wider drain buffers.
- **Supreme Court, 5 March 2019:** set the NGT's buffer direction aside except against the two
  respondents in that case (paras 61, 62(ii)). The Court did not rule on whether 30 m is
  enough. In general, the RMP's 30 m stands.
- **Karnataka Act No. 19 of 2026** (assent 16 February 2026, gazetted 18 February 2026)
  replaces s. 12(3) of the Tank Conservation and Development Authority Act 2014: no
  construction or commercial, recreational or industrial activity within a buffer measured
  from the tank's revenue boundary, graded by the tank's area (acres; the table's small sizes
  are in the acre.gunta notation):

  | Tank area | Buffer |
  |---|---:|
  | up to 0.05 | 0 m |
  | 0.05-0.10 | 1 m |
  | 0.10-1 acre | 3 m |
  | 1-10 acres | 6 m |
  | 10-25 acres | 12 m |
  | 25-100 acres | 24 m |
  | over 100 acres | 30 m |

  Roads, bridges, power, water and drainage lines, sewage plants and similar public utilities
  may enter it with the Authority's prior approval, if inflow and outflow are not obstructed.
- The Act amends the tank authority's own prohibition; it does not amend the RMP's zoning
  regulations, and the October 2025 drain notification (below) said lake buffers stay as per
  revenue records. Both apply to a plan, so the stricter binds: **30 m** for every tank.

The site's tanks, by mapped water area (Overture; the revenue boundary is usually larger):
Kaikondrahalli 16.5 ha (41 acres), Saul Kere 16.5 ha (41 acres), Kasavanahalli 12.7 ha
(31 acres), Haralur 7.9 ha (20 acres). Under the 2026 Act alone they would take 24 m (the
first three) and 12 or 24 m (Haralur, depending on its revenue area).

Confidence: high that the RMP sets 30 m and that the Act grades the tank authority's buffer
as above (both read in the documents); medium that 30 m is what an approval applies today
(how the two are reconciled in practice was not found in writing).

## Drains (rajakaluves)

- **RMP 2015 (note to 4.12.2):** primary, secondary and tertiary drains keep 50, 25 and 15 m
  either side, measured from the drain's centre.
- **NGT 2016:** 50, 35 and 25 m from the edge; set aside in general by the Supreme Court in
  2019, as above.
- **October 2025:** a final notification of the Urban Development Department (amending the
  zoning regulations under s. 13-E of the KTCP Act) halved the drain buffers across the
  Bengaluru Metropolitan Region (Deccan Herald, 16 October 2025). The notification and its
  figures were not found; halving the RMP's figures gives 25, 12.5 and 7.5 m, **assumed**.

## Using these numbers in Permutation City

- Lakes: the rule is 30 m (forbidden ground, shared). The design default is 75 m, the NGT's
  figure, labelled as a design choice beyond the legal minimum; a setting moves it between
  30 and 150 m. The capacity is shown at both.
- Drains: the rule is 25, 12.5 and 7.5 m (assumed, above), with stream orders 3 and over,
  2 and 1 read as primary, secondary and tertiary (assumed: the RMP's drain classes are
  drawn on its maps, not defined by stream order). The design default keeps the RMP's
  original 50, 25 and 15 m, labelled as a design choice.
