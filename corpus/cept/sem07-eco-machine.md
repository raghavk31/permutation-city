## The site and the problem

The Eastern Waterfront runs along Mumbai's docks (Prince's, Victoria and Indira docks,
Masjid Bunder, the fish market, P D'Mello Road), with oil and chemical works, ship
breaking and the Koliwada community. The studio framed two problems: waste reaching the sea
through drains and creeks, and flooding. As presented, Mumbai averages about 2,142 mm of
rain a year, 96 per cent of it in the monsoon; the storm-water drains can take about 25 mm of
rain; low-lying drains match the flood spots; and about 40 per cent of the city could face
regular flooding by 2050 with sea-level rise.

## The proposal

The site becomes a transition between land and sea. Infrastructure goes underground (flood
reservoir, water treatment, waste-to-energy, recycling) with public programmes on top.
Public wetlands act as a buffer, and floating modules collect waste from the water and
carry recreational, cultural and sports uses, spread across the site rather than
centralised.

## The method

A 20 m hex grid (a 40 m square grid was tried too), streams used as attractors to spread
water across the land, unit scale factors of 0.3 to 0.9, programmes by unit size (under
100, 100 to 350, 350 to 700 and 700 to 850 m²), and heights from sun and wind in bands of
0, 1 to 3, 3 to 6, 6 to 10 and 10 to 15 levels.

## Using this in Permutation City

Water shares at the high end (up to 30 per cent), water that follows the site's own
drainage and flood lines (the follow-water gene near 1), heights mostly under 10 to 15
levels, and flood storage as a leading objective.
