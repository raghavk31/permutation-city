## How typologies are used

The 20 m cell grid can't draw a courtyard: each cell is one use and one height. So a
typology works at two levels. At site scale it is a prior on the density pattern, a
starting population biased towards its kind of fabric (labelled as such and optional). At
building scale (Phase 7) it becomes a parametric form inside each plot's envelope.

## Single courtyard

A perimeter block around a central court. Sem 06: a 60 m block, the court offset 12 to
18 m in from the edge (576 to 1,296 m² of court), unit cells of 3 to 7.5 m, floor plates
every 3 m, low (1 to 3 floors), medium (3 to 5) and high (5 to 8). Shades its court, keeps
open space inside the block, suits housing. At site scale: even, mid-rise heights, high
coverage, little difference between cores and the background.

## L-shaped

Two wings at a corner, open on two sides. Sem 06 primitive. More light and air than a
closed court, an open corner for access. At site scale: mid-rise, medium coverage.

## U-shaped

Three wings around a court open on one side. Sem 06 primitive. The open side faces a
street, view or breeze. At site scale: like the courtyard, a little lower coverage.

## Tower

A tall, slender building with open ground around it. Sem 06 primitive (up to 24 m in the
block study). High floor area on a small footprint, but long shadows and little street
frontage; "towers in a park" was the failure mode of Permutation City's first runs. At
site scale: low coverage, heights concentrated at cores (a high peak), low background
density.

## Slab

A long, thin block, often 10 to 15 m deep so every flat has light on two sides (author's
description; no corpus document). Efficient, but long slabs block breezes and views. At
site scale: mid to high-rise, medium coverage, moderate concentration.

## Podium and tower

A low podium (parking, shops, services) covering most of the plot, with one or more
towers above. Both regulations provide for podiums. In Ahmedabad (CGDCR 6.14.2) a podium
needs a plot of at least 4,000 m², may be at most 10 m high, and keeps 6 m from the plot
boundary for fire tenders. Mumbai lets up to 40 per cent of layout open space sit on one. Busy street edges and dense floor area, but sealed ground. At
site scale: high coverage, high peak, low background.
